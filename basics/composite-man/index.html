The Composite Man

Who is on the other side of your trade? Wyckoff's answer: picture all the big money as one man. He buys when the crowd is scared, sells when the crowd is excited, and repeats. Learn to think like him, and you stop being his customer.

▶ The lesson video is on its way.

1. Who is the Composite Man?

Funds, banks, insiders and big traders move the market. Each has its own plans, but they act alike: they buy large positions when prices are low and sell them when prices are high. Wyckoff told his students to treat all of them as one big operator, the Composite Man. It is a way of thinking, not a real person: when you see a chart, ask what he is doing.

He is too big to buy or sell all at once. If he bought everything in one day, he would push the price up against himself. So he works slowly, inside ranges, and that is why his footprints show up in price ranges and volume.

2. His campaign: four stages

Every campaign follows the same cycle you met in lesson 1. This time, watch it from his side.

Stage 1 · Accumulation: he buys in fear

After a long fall the news is bad and people sell to get out. He quietly buys their shares at cheap prices, inside a range, without chasing the price up. Smart money is buying. The clue: big volume, but price stops falling.

Stage 2 · Mark-up: price climbs

Once he holds most of the shares there are few sellers left, so even modest buying lifts the price. The crowd notices late and starts to chase.

Stage 3 · Distribution: he sells into greed

At the top the news is great and the crowd rushes in. He sells them his shares, again slowly, inside a range. Smart money is selling. The clue: big volume, but price stops rising.

Stage 4 · Mark-down: price falls

With his selling done, buyers run out and price falls. The crowd that bought the top now panics, and the stage is set for his next round of buying.

3. Buy cheap, sell high, repeat

Then the cycle starts again. The crowd buys high and sells low because it follows the news; he does the opposite because he follows supply and demand. Same chart, opposite sides.

The crowd
Sells in fear near the lows, buys in excitement near the highs. Follows the news.
The Composite Man
Buys the crowd's fear, sells into the crowd's greed. Follows supply and demand.

4. Trade with him, not against him

You can't see his orders, but you can see his footprints: ranges after long moves, volume that doesn't match the price, and tests like the spring, where price dips under the range and snaps back because he is still buying. Our trades are built on exactly that: find where he is accumulating, and buy alongside him.

YOUR EDGE

Before every trade, ask: is the Composite Man buying here, or selling? Be on his side, or stay out.

What to remember

  1. The Composite Man is all the big money pictured as one operator.
  2. He buys in fear (accumulation), lets price rise (mark-up), sells into greed (distribution), and lets it fall (mark-down). Then he repeats.
  3. He is too big to hide: his footprints show in ranges and volume. Trade with him, not against him.

Quick check

1. The news is terrible and everyone is selling, yet price stops falling on huge volume. What is the Composite Man doing?

Buying: he is accumulating the shares the scared crowd is selling.

2. Why does he buy slowly inside a range instead of all at once?

He is so big that buying all at once would push the price up against himself.

3. After a long rise, the crowd is excited and buying. Which stage is he most likely in?

Distribution: he is selling his shares to the excited crowd.
