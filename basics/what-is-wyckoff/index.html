What is Wyckoff?

Wyckoff shows you where smart money is moving, so you can be on the same side. In this lesson: how markets move, market structure, the market cycle, accumulation and distribution, the Composite Man and the three laws.

1. Three ways a market moves

Every market, at any moment, is doing one of three things. It is falling (a mark-down), rising (a mark-up), or moving sideways in a horizontal range. That's all. Wyckoff's insight was that the sideways part is not random: it is where the next trend is prepared.

2. Market structure

Market structure is the shape price makes as it moves. Read it from the highs and lows:

  • Mark-down: lower highs and lower lows. Each bounce fails below the last one.
  • Mark-up: higher highs and higher lows. Each dip holds above the last one.
  • Range: price is capped above and supported below. Neither side is winning yet.

Trends begin and end in ranges. That is why Wyckoff traders spend most of their time studying them.

3. The market cycle

Put the pieces together and you get the market cycle, in four phases that repeat:

  1. Accumulation: a range after a fall, where big money buys.
  2. Mark-up: the rise, once big money holds most of the shares.
  3. Distribution: a range after a rise, where big money sells.
  4. Mark-down: the fall, once big money has sold. Then it starts again.

4. Accumulation and distribution

Accumulation: after a long fall, people who are scared or tired give up and sell. Big money buys those shares, slowly, inside a range, so it doesn't push the price up too early. The tell is volume: at the selling climax volume jumps (3.2 times normal on DEMO), yet price stops falling. Huge selling met by an even bigger buyer.

Distribution is the opposite. After a long rise, people are excited and keen to buy. Big money sells them its shares, again slowly, inside a range. At the buying climax volume jumps (3.2 times normal on DEMO), yet price stops rising. Huge buying, absorbed by an even bigger seller.

5. The Composite Man

Wyckoff told his students to picture all big money as one trader: the Composite Man. He plans his campaigns, he buys when the crowd is scared (accumulation) and sells when the crowd is excited (distribution). He is too big to buy or sell all at once, so his work shows up as ranges and volume. Lesson 2 covers him in full.

YOUR EDGE

Wyckoff teaches you to see where smart money is moving, so you can be on the same side: buying when it buys, and staying out of the way when it sells.

6. The three laws

The Composite Man leaves footprints that follow three laws:

Supply and demand
More buyers than sellers: price rises. More sellers than buyers: it falls.
Cause and effect
The range is the cause; the trend after it is the effect. A longer range usually means a bigger move.
Effort versus result
Volume is the effort, price movement is the result. When they disagree, someone big is at work.

Each of these gets its own lesson later in the course.

What to remember

  1. Wyckoff shows where smart money is moving, so you can be on the same side.
  2. Markets fall, rise or move sideways; ranges are where big money buys or sells.
  3. The Composite Man and the three laws explain the footprints. More in the next lessons.

Quick check

1. Price makes higher highs and higher lows. Which phase is that?

A mark-up.

2. After a long fall, volume explodes but price stops falling. What is big money probably doing?

Buying: absorbing the panic selling. That is how accumulation starts.

3. Which law says the longer the range, the bigger the move after it?

Cause and effect.
